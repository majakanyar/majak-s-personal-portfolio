Sales Training Redesign - Telecommunications Account
Agents knew the product but couldn't convert on live calls. Conversion flat, AHT inflated, client escalating. Rebuilt around real QA failure data - conversion +10%, sales +13% in a single quarter.

Challenge
Agents could recite the full product catalogue but couldn't close. Conversion stagnant. Call time running long because agents were improvising through objections without a structural framework. The client had been tracking the numbers for two quarters. Pressure was building.
Approach
Programme rebuilt around real call behaviour - objection patterns extracted from QA data, not hypothetical scenarios. Storyline simulations tied directly to CRM workflows replaced passive content delivery. Daily coaching cadence during nesting reinforced trained behaviours before they had time to decay.
Outcome & Business Impact
Sales conversion increased 10% within the quarter. Overall sales grew 13%. Call time dropped 50 seconds. Client extended scope. Programme became the account standard. A 3-week redesign investment produced measurable commercial returns within one quarter.
5 Whys - Why were agents failing to convert on live calls?
W1
Why was conversion flat despite product knowledge?
Agents weren't guiding customers toward a decision - calls ended without a close attempt. No framework for doing so.
W2
Why didn't agents have a conversation framework?
Training focused entirely on product knowledge. How to navigate a live sales call was never designed into the programme.
W3
Why was the programme only covering product knowledge?
Curriculum built around what the business wanted to teach, not what agents needed to do on the floor.
W4
Why wasn't the curriculum mapped to floor performance?
QA data showing where agents were failing had never been fed back into training design.
W5
Why wasn't QA data used in training design?
L&D and QA operated in separate silos. No shared review process. Nobody owned the connection between the two.

Challenge
A global logistics operation with consistent CSAT pressure. Agents passing training - completion rates fine. On the floor, system usage inconsistent and CSAT underperforming. The standard diagnosis was more training. The actual problem was structural.
Approach
Programme rebuilt around patterns extracted from live QA failure data. System navigation replicas built for zero-risk practice. A nesting model introduced formal shared accountability between L&D and operations - the first time these two functions shared responsibility on this account.
Outcome & Business Impact
CSAT improved 9 percentage points. System usage increased 25%. The nesting model was adopted globally by the logistics client. A 5-week investment produced an operational model the client scaled worldwide.
5 Whys
W1
Why was CSAT below target despite agents completing onboarding?
Wrong system steps, inconsistent resolution paths on live calls.
W2
Why were interactions being handled incorrectly?
Gap between training content and real floor demands - skills trained didn't match skills required.
W3
Why was there a gap between training and floor demands?
Training designed from product documentation, not analysis of what agents were failing at in their first 30 days.
W4
Why wasn't failure data used to design training?
QA and L&D operated in separate silos with no formal process for failure data to feed into curriculum design.
W5
Why did these functions operate in silos?
No shared accountability structure. Each team tracked its own KPI. Nobody owned the full new hire performance journey.

Challenge
A 6-week programme running unchanged for over a year. Nobody had measured content against actual floor performance. The cost was significant - 6 weeks of salary before meaningful contribution, operational bottlenecks during peak hiring, pressure on existing staff.
Approach
A Kirkpatrick L1–L4 audit identified 40% of classroom time covered content agents never accessed in their first 30 days. Curriculum rebuilt backwards from a 30-day performance map - every module traced to a specific on-floor behaviour. Daily micro-coaching during nesting prevented skill decay.
Outcome & Business Impact
Time to full performance dropped from 6 to 3.5 weeks - 42% reduction across four cohorts. Error rate fell 15%. At 200+ starters per month, saving 2.5 weeks per person compounds into significant recurring return. Adopted as the standard across all accounts.
5 Whys
W1
Why were new starters taking 6 weeks to reach minimum performance?
Programme contained content unrelated to what starters needed in their first 30 days.
W2
Why did the programme contain irrelevant content?
Curriculum never reviewed against actual floor performance data - designed and left unchanged.
W3
Why hadn't the curriculum been reviewed?
No regular audit process. L&D owned completion figures, not performance outcomes.
W4
Why did L&D own completion and not outcomes?
Success was defined as headcount graduating. Speed to performance was never a measurable target.
W5
Why wasn't speed to performance a target?
The cost of the 6-week timeline was never quantified. Nobody calculated what 2.5 extra weeks of salary per person per month cost at scale.

L&D Digital Transformation - Classroom-to-eLearning Migration
Every session required a trainer and a room. Nothing reusable. Costs scaled linearly with headcount. Built the business case, secured buy-in, designed 70+ digital modules, deployed through SuccessFactors LMS. Costs fell 40% permanently.

Challenge
Every new starter needed a trainer's physical time and a room. Nothing reusable. Content quality varied by individual. As headcount grew, training cost base grew proportionally. Nobody had flagged this as a structural problem. I identified it, calculated the cost at scale, and initiated the transformation before being asked to.
Approach
Built the ROI business case and secured sign-off from operations leadership and the client. Personally designed 70+ modules in Rise 360 and Articulate 360 - interactive branching scenarios, gamified knowledge checks, self-paced foundational content. Deployed through SuccessFactors LMS with a train-the-trainer programme.
Outcome & Business Impact
Training costs fell 40% permanently. A library of 70+ reusable assets built from zero. Delivery consistency improved across all accounts. The operation could now grow headcount without growing the training cost base at the same rate.
5 Whys
W1
Why did training costs scale linearly with headcount?
Every new starter required a trainer's physical time - no reusable content existed.
W2
Why was there no reusable content?
Everything delivered live and verbally - nothing built into digital modules.
W3
Why hadn't anything been built digitally?
L&D team lacked authoring skills and tools, and no budget had been allocated.
W4
Why hadn't budget been allocated?
Leadership viewed training as an operational cost, not a scalable asset. ROI of digital transformation had never been presented.
W5
Why had the ROI never been presented?
Nobody in L&D had calculated the cost of the current model at scale, let alone built the business case to change it.
