Role: Training Supervisor · Teleperformance · Oct 2022 – Apr 2024

Challenge
A global logistics operation with consistent CSAT pressure. Agents passing training but arriving on the floor underprepared. Tool usage inconsistent and CSAT underperforming. The standard diagnosis was more training. The actual problem was structural.
Approach
Programme rebuilt around patterns extracted from live QA data. System navigation replicas built for practice. A new-hire coaching model introduced formal shared responsibility between training and operations - the first time these two functions shared accountability on this account.
Outcome & Business Impact
Across the programs in this role, CSAT rose 7 to 10 percent, tool usage rose 25% and AHT fell 30 seconds. The coaching model was adopted globally by the logistics client.
5 Whys
W1
Why was CSAT below target despite agents completing onboarding?
Inconsistent system navigation and resolution paths on live calls.
W2
Why were interactions being handled incorrectly?
A gap between training content and real floor demands - the skills trained didn't match the skills required.
W3
Why was there a gap between training and floor demands?
Training was designed from product documentation, not from analysis of what agents were struggling with in their first weeks.
W4
Why wasn't floor performance data used to design training?
QA and training operated in separate silos with no formal process for feeding failure data into curriculum design.
W5
Why did these functions operate in silos?
No shared accountability structure. Each team tracked its own KPI. Nobody owned the full new-hire performance journey.

Challenge
Agents could recite the product catalogue but couldn't close. Conversion was flat because agents were improvising through objections without a structural framework.
Approach
Training rebuilt around live call practice - objection patterns extracted from QA data, not hypothetical scenarios. Simulations tied to real workflows replaced passive content delivery. Follow-up coaching on the floor reinforced trained behaviours.
Outcome & Business Impact
Conversion rose 10% and the client extended scope.
5 Whys - Why were agents failing to convert on live calls?
W1
Why was conversion flat despite product knowledge?
Agents weren't guiding customers toward a decision - calls ended without a close attempt. No framework for doing so.
W2
Why didn't agents have a conversation framework?
Training focused entirely on product knowledge. How to navigate a live sales call was never designed into the programme.
W3
Why was the programme only covering product knowledge?
Curriculum built around what the business wanted to teach, not what agents needed to do on the floor.
W4
Why wasn't the curriculum mapped to floor performance?
QA data showing where agents were failing had never been fed back into training design.
W5
Why wasn't QA data used in training design?
Training and QA operated in separate silos. No shared review process. Nobody owned the connection between the two.

A 6-week programme running unchanged for a long time. Nobody had measured its content against actual floor performance.
Approach
Curriculum rebuilt backwards from what new starters actually did in their first weeks on the floor. Every module was traced to a specific on-floor behaviour. Micro-coaching during nesting prevented skill decay.

Ramp time fell from 6 to 3.5 weeks. Error rate fell 15%.
5 Whys

Why were new starters taking 6 weeks to reach minimum performance?
The programme contained content unrelated to what starters needed in their first weeks.
W2
Why did the programme contain irrelevant content?
Curriculum was never reviewed against actual floor performance - designed once and left unchanged.
W3
Why hadn't the curriculum been reviewed?
No regular review process. Training owned completion figures, not performance outcomes.
W4
Why did training own completion and not outcomes?
Success was defined as headcount graduating. Speed to performance was never a measurable target.
W5
Why wasn't speed to performance a target?
The cost of the 6-week timeline was never quantified.

Cutting training costs 40 to 50% by going digital: Classroom to eLearning
Role: Operations Training Manager · Teleperformance · Apr 2024 – Feb 2025
Every session required a trainer and a room, with nothing reusable. Proposed the move to digital, wrote the business case, got sign-off and built 70+ modules.

Challenge
Every new starter needed a trainer's physical time and a room. Nothing was reusable. As headcount grew, the training cost base grew with it. Nobody had flagged this as a structural problem.
Approach
Proposed the move from classroom to digital training, wrote the business case, and got operations and client sign-off. Built 70+ modules in Rise 360 and Articulate 360, deployed through SuccessFactors LMS with a train-the-trainer programme.
Outcome & Business Impact
Training costs fell 40 to 50%. A library of 70+ reusable digital modules was built. The operation could grow headcount without growing the training cost base at the same rate.
5 Whys
W1
Why did training costs scale with headcount?
Every new starter required a trainer's physical time - no reusable content existed.
W2
Why was there no reusable content?
Everything was delivered live and verbally - nothing built into digital modules.
W3
Why hadn't anything been built digitally?
The training team lacked authoring tools and no budget had been allocated.
W4
Why hadn't budget been allocated?
Leadership viewed training as an operational cost, not a scalable asset. The ROI of going digital had never been presented.
W5
Why had the ROI never been presented?
Nobody had built the business case to change the model.
